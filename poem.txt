At 2:13 the ferry noses the slip,
its deck empty but for rain and my boots.
I give the agent in the console
wind speed, draft, and the tide's turn.
On its screen, a green bar: HOLD.

Below, the winch takes the line.
Wet hemp rasps through the guide;
the motor keeps its patient pull
whether I mean to leave
or only mean to stop being afraid.

It turns weather into arrows,
tide into a row of seconds.
But the red release stays under my thumb—
a small, warm door
no forecast can open.

I wait until the water stops worrying
at the stern. Then I let the line go.

It drops in a dark loop,
weight remembering the deck,
then giving it back.

The ferry turns toward the far lights.
Behind me, the winch unwinds its empty pull.
On the screen, arrows still cross
the water where the rope was.
